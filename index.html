School Multipliers: growing a student research olympiad through Indian schools

A global research olympiad for 13 to 18 year olds already reaches students in 89+ countries, mostly one student at a time. Schools change that: one partner school can register a whole group, and one school network can open dozens of schools. This page maps the Indian networks that could bring schools in, why each would say yes, how I'd approach a school, and how I'd track it for a board.

School outreach playbook

Schools rarely say yes to a cold brochure. They say yes when the right person sees a clear benefit for their students and very little extra work.

A three-touch sequence

Touch 1 · Email or WhatsApp to the right contact

Dear Ms Sharma, your students already do research for the Extended Essay. Our research olympiad tests exactly those skills: reading a paper, interpreting data, forming a research question. The top 40% of participants receive named honours. Could I send a free practice paper for your Diploma students to try?

Touch 2 · Five working days later, with the practice paper

Sharing the practice paper and answer key as promised. If even a few students try it, I'd be glad to hear how they found it. Schools that register a group get one coordinator login and a single results report.

Touch 3 · A short call or visit

Fifteen minutes with the coordinator to agree three things: a named teacher coordinator, sharing registration with eligible classes, and one practice session before the online round.

The names and wording are examples. In practice I'd adapt each message to the school type in the table above.

What a "signed partner school" means

  • A named teacher coordinator who receives updates and the school's results report.
  • Registration shared with all eligible classes, not one student.
  • One practice session using the free mock exam before the online round.
  • Permission to list the school as a partner, and to share winners' stories with consent.

What I'd ask teachers first

Before scaling outreach, I'd run five short conversations with teachers and counsellors and change the plan based on what they say.

  1. How does your school decide which competitions to promote, and who decides?
  2. When in the year is it easiest to add one?
  3. What makes you say no to a competition?
  4. Would a group discount or a school results report change your answer?
  5. Who else at your school should hear about this?

The first 90 days

Days 1–30 · Learn and set up

  • Pull last cycle's Indian registrations by school and city to see where students already come from.
  • Five conversations with teachers and counsellors, using the questions above.
  • Build the school kit: principal note, practice paper, club guide and tracked referral links.
  • Start with the "Now" groups: IB schools, IC3 counsellors and ambassadors.

Days 31–60 · First partners

  • Run the three-touch sequence with the first 60 schools and track every reply.
  • Open a conversation with one private chain's central office.
  • Give Indian ambassadors the school kit and a simple monthly target.

Days 61–90 · Prove it and widen

  • Replace the funnel assumptions with real rates and share them with leadership.
  • Approach the "Next" groups with results from the first partner schools.
  • Write the playbook so the same approach can run in other countries.
